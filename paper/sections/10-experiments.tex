\section{Experiments}\label{sec:exp}

This section measures the implementation of \cref{sec:impl}.
The goals are specific: to compare Kronecker-FRI with the kernel-basis scheme of~\cite{Mkh26} and with a coefficient-form baseline at identical engineering, to measure the effect of folding arity and Merkle caps, to locate where the prover's time goes, and to quantify salting and the number of folding rounds.
All codes are single-threaded and use no SIMD and no hash-specific batching, so that the comparison isolates the protocols.

\subsection{Setup}\label{sec:exp:setup}

\paragraph{Machine.}
All runs use one core of an Intel Xeon processor at $2.10$\,GHz (cloud virtual machine, $2$ vCPUs, $7$\,GB RAM), Rust $1.95.0$, release profile with fat LTO and one codegen unit, and no \texttt{target-cpu} flag.
All numbers of this section come from one session on one machine; they are not comparable with the absolute timings reported in~\cite{Mkh26}, which were measured on another machine.
Times are medians over $11$ runs for $n \le 18$, $5$ for $n = 20$ and $3$ for $n = 22$ and for \cref{tab:exp-arity,tab:exp-stop,tab:apps-params}; verification times are medians over $21$ runs.
The machine is a shared virtual machine, and timings of identical work vary by up to about $10$--$20\%$ between runs.

\paragraph{Parameters.}
Unless stated otherwise: $\rho = 1/4$ ($R = 2$), $\kappa = 148$, the quadratic extension $\F = \mathbb{F}_{q^2}$, and unsalted Merkle trees. We use two configurations of Kronecker-FRI:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{KF-2}: arity $2$, no caps, $\ell = n - 4$ (final polynomial of $16$ coefficients), the configuration of the schemes of~\cite{Mkh26}, for the comparison at identical engineering;
  \item \emph{KF-8}: arity $8$, caps of $128$ nodes, $\ell = n - 8$ (final polynomial of $256$ coefficients), the configuration we recommend (\cref{sec:exp:arity,sec:exp:stop}).
\end{itemize}
Both deduplicate openings (\cref{sec:impl:merkle}).
By \cref{thm:sound} with $\delta = \delta^* = 3/8$, the query term is $(5/8)^{148} < 2^{-100}$, and the field term is below $2^{-100}$ for all sizes tested ($M \le 2^{24}$; \cref{tab:field}).
Polynomials have uniformly random coefficients in $\Fq$, and $z$ is uniformly random in $\F^n$.

\paragraph{Schemes compared.}
We compare three schemes that share the field arithmetic, the NTT, the BLAKE3 Merkle trees with fibre leaves and the transcript, and differ only in how an evaluation is proved.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{Kronecker-FRI}: $\Pi_{\mathrm{KF}}$ as implemented in \cref{sec:impl}.
  \item \emph{KBFold}: the evaluation protocol of~\cite{Mkh26}, from its reference implementation: the table $f$ is encoded in the Boolean-kernel basis, and a sumcheck of $\ell$ rounds runs alongside the kernel folds.
  \item \emph{Coefficient-form baseline}: the second mode of the same implementation~\cite[\S8]{Mkh26}, which encodes the coefficient form of $f$ and folds with the classical FRI fold, alongside the same sumcheck. This is the coefficient-form folding of Reed--Solomon instantiations of BaseFold~\cite{ZCF24}; it is \emph{not} the BaseFold code base.
\end{itemize}
The implementation of~\cite{Mkh26} uses unsalted trees, arity $2$, no caps and no deduplication, so the comparison uses unsalted trees for Kronecker-FRI too; salting is measured separately in \cref{sec:exp:salt}.
All three schemes are run with the same $\rho$, $\ell$ and $\kappa$, which give the same query term; for the two sumcheck-based schemes the field term is $\sum_j (M_j + 2)/|\F|$~\cite{Mkh26}, and for Kronecker-FRI it is $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{KF}} - (1-\delta)^\kappa < 3M/|\F|$, both below $2^{-100}$ here.

\paragraph{Commit.}
The three schemes perform the same work up to the transform that precedes the NTT: the Boolean-kernel transform for KBFold, the M\"obius transform for the baseline, and nothing for Kronecker-FRI, which we run from the coefficient form. From a table, Kronecker-FRI also performs the M\"obius transform, which takes $12$\,ms at $n = 20$ and $57$\,ms at $n = 22$ in the same session (the transform column of the baseline), below $2\%$ of the commitment. For $n \ge 14$ the commitment times agree within $15\%$, in both directions; at $n = 12$ the timings, of $2$ to $3$\,ms, are dominated by noise.

\paragraph{Verify.}
Verification times agree within the variability of the machine, between $2.2$ and $7.2$\,ms: the three verifiers are dominated by the authentication paths. Kronecker-FRI replaces the sumcheck checks by the computation of $K_z(\pm\xi)$ and $h(\pm\xi)$ at each query point, $O(n)$ field operations per point (\cref{prop:costs}(3)), which is not visible at this resolution.

\paragraph{Proof size.}
Kronecker-FRI opens one more tree, that of $w_A$, at every query point, while the $\ell$ sumcheck messages of the other schemes are negligible. With deduplication, which saves $56$ to $59$\,KiB here, Kronecker-FRI proofs are within $1.4$--$4.2\%$ of those of KBFold ($5$ to $54$\,KiB larger); without it, the difference is the $\kappa$ openings of $w_A$, $\kappa\,(32(\log_2 M - 1) + 32)$ bytes minus the sumcheck messages ($101$\,KiB at $n = 20$).

\paragraph{Open.}
Opening is $2.5$ to $3.5$ times slower for Kronecker-FRI with arity $2$ than for KBFold. The prover commits to one more word of length $M$ over $\F$; \cref{sec:exp:breakdown} measures each step.

\Cref{tab:exp-arity} varies the arity and the cap at $n = 20$; by \cref{prop:arity}, every cell has the soundness error of \cref{thm:sound}.
Arity $2^k$ divides the number of trees of folded words by $k$, and the paths opened per query with them, while each opened group grows to $2^{k-1}$ leaves; caps of $2^7$ nodes remove the $7$ top levels of every path, which the $148$ queries share.
Together they reduce the proof by a factor of $3.4$ to $3.6$ with respect to arity $2$ without caps: from $1117$ to $315$\,KiB at $\ell = 16$ and from $1008$ to $299$\,KiB at $\ell = 12$.
With caps, arities $8$ and $16$ give the smallest proofs, within $3\%$ of each other, and arity $8$ verifies faster than arity $16$, which folds $15$ values per committed word and query; arity $4$ verifies fastest, with proofs $23$--$24\%$ larger.
Opening time is lower for larger arities (about $2.2$\,s for arity $2$, $1.4$--$1.8$\,s for arities $8$ and $16$), since fewer trees are built; commitment time does not depend on it.
These optimisations are generic: they apply to every FRI-type test, including those of KBFold and of the baseline, whose implementation does not use them; the comparison at identical engineering is that of \cref{tab:exp-scaling}.

\Cref{tab:exp-breakdown} splits the KF-8 opening into its steps; the components add up to the measured total within the variability of the machine ($-5\%$ to $+6\%$).
The last column, the folds and the trees of the committed levels, is the work of the folding test itself; with arity $8$ it is between $9.8\%$ and $12.7\%$ of the opening.
The other four columns are the commitment to the opening polynomial:
\begin{itemize}
  \item the product $U_f K_z$, with $2nN$ multiplications in $\F$ (\cref{lem:cost}(3));
  \item the NTT of $A$ over $\F$, which costs $e = 2$ base-field NTTs of size $M$ (\cref{fact:ntt});
  \item the Merkle tree of $w_A$, with $M/2$ leaves of two elements of $\F$;
  \item the words $\ev_L(K_z)$, $h$ and $w_0$, with $O(M)$ operations in $\F$ (\cref{prop:costs}(2)).
\end{itemize}
Each costs between $0.3$ and $0.85$ of a commitment, and together $1.9$ to $2.6$ commitments. In exchange, the verifier runs no sumcheck and the soundness argument has a single algebraic step (\cref{lem:batching}).
The asymptotic cost of the opening is $O(M\log M)$; the product, the NTT and the tree of $w_A$ are independent passes that parallelise like the commitment.

\subsection{Salting}\label{sec:exp:salt}

Salting every leaf with $32$ bytes (\cref{tab:exp-kf8}) costs $21$--$39\%$ in commit time and $17$--$38\%$ in open time (the salts are derived by one keyed BLAKE3 call per leaf), and adds $32$ bytes per opened leaf and per round to the proof, $41$ to $102$\,KiB; it does not change the verification time beyond the variability of the machine.
The post-quantum statement (\cref{cor:pq}) requires salts; \cref{tab:apps-params} measures the salted parameter sets.
The commitment is unaffected by the choice of $\F$, since $\Enc(f)$ is computed over $\Fq$.

We use $\ell = n - 8$, the smallest proofs at $n = 20$.

\subsection{Summary}\label{sec:exp:summary}

At identical engineering, Kronecker-FRI commits and verifies as fast as KBFold and as the coefficient-form baseline, with proofs within $5\%$ of theirs, and its opening pays for the commitment to $A$.
With arity $8$ and Merkle caps, which \cref{prop:arity} covers without changing the soundness error, proofs are $95$ to $372$\,KiB and verification $1.6$ to $4.5$\,ms for $n = 12, \dots, 22$, unsalted, with interactive soundness error below $2^{-99}$, and opening is $1.2$ to $1.4$ times faster than with arity $2$.
The protocol has no sumcheck: its verifier is an FRI verifier with one local computation per query, and its soundness reduces to the folding test and one application of correlated agreement for curves of degree $2$.
\Cref{sec:apps} gives parameter sets and compares with other schemes.
