\section{Verification Status}\label{app:verification}

This appendix records, for each result, how it is established.
\emph{Proved} means proved in this paper; \emph{from~\cite{Mkh26}} means that the statement and its proof are reproduced from the companion paper; \emph{adapted} means that the proof follows one of~\cite{Mkh26} with changes; \emph{quoted} means that the result is cited and not reproved.

\paragraph{Lean~4.}
The results of \cref{sec:kernel,sec:fold,sec:scheme,sec:sound} and of \cref{sec:apps:batch} are formalised in Lean~4 with Mathlib, in the directory \texttt{lean/} of the companion repository, without \texttt{sorry}.
The formalisation has a single axiom, \cref{thm:curves} (\texttt{KBFold.bciks\_curves}); the case $t = 1$ used by the folding test is derived from it.
A continuous-integration job builds the project, checks that no \texttt{sorry} occurs and that \texttt{bciks\_curves} is the only axiom, and prints the axioms on which every main theorem depends.
The column \emph{Lean} gives the name of the main Lean statement; the file \texttt{lean/README.md} gives the complete correspondence.
The formalisation models a deterministic prover by its messages as functions of the challenges, with the hypotheses that a message depends only on the earlier challenges and that the final polynomial has fewer than $N_\ell$ coefficients; in the generic model of \cref{def:iop}, used for \cref{thm:rbr}, neither hypothesis is needed.
Operation counts and the base-field statements are established in the paper and checked by the test suite.
The modules shared with~\cite{Mkh26} (fibre distance, decoding, good challenges, probability lemmas and the framework of \cref{sec:proofs}) are those of its Lean project.

\emph{Tested} refers to the Rust test suite and examples of \cref{sec:impl:tests}, which check the statement on random instances; tests are evidence, not proofs.
