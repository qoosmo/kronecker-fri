\title{Kronecker-FRI: A Hash-Based Multilinear Polynomial Commitment\\ from a Coefficient-Extraction Identity}
\author{Abdelali Mkhida\\ Algorizk Labs\\ \texttt{ali.mkhida@algorizk.xyz}}
\date{Preprint, September 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle
\begin{abstract}
We present Kronecker-FRI, a transparent, hash-based polynomial commitment scheme for multilinear polynomials whose evaluation proofs need no sumcheck.
The scheme rests on the extraction identity $f(z) = [X^{N-1}]\,U_f(X)K_z(X)$, where $U_f$ is the Kronecker encoding of $f$ and $K_z(X) = \prod_{k=1}^{n}(X^{2^{k-1}} + z_k)$: a multilinear evaluation is one coefficient of a univariate product.
The prover commits to $U_f$ as a Reed--Solomon codeword. To open at $z$, it commits to a single opening polynomial; the verifier derives a second one pointwise as a virtual word, batches the words with one challenge, and runs an FRI-style folding test, so that one proximity test enforces both low degree and the claimed value.
We prove completeness, soundness, round-by-round knowledge soundness and evaluation binding, for any folding arity $2^k$, with correlated agreement for Reed--Solomon codes as the only external result; we derive post-quantum straightline knowledge soundness of the compiled scheme in the quantum random oracle model; and we extend the scheme to batched openings of several polynomials.
All interactive results are machine-checked in Lean~4, with correlated agreement as the single axiom.
A Rust implementation over the Goldilocks field commits and verifies as fast as a sumcheck-based Reed--Solomon scheme at identical engineering; with arity $8$ and Merkle caps it opens a polynomial with $2^{20}$ coefficients at a $100$-bit interactive soundness level with a $299$\,KiB proof, verified in $3.4$\,ms on a single core.
\end{abstract}
