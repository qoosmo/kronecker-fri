\begin{abstract}
All protocols of this note are built on the hash-based commitment Kronecker-FRI and inherit its post-quantum security basis. We encode a multilinear polynomial by its \emph{table}, $V_f(X)=\sum_{w}f(w)X^{w}$, instead of its coefficients.
Two quantities then become single coefficients of univariate products: the evaluation $\tilde f(z)$, with a product-form kernel, and the hypercube inner product $\sum_{w}a(w)b(w)$ of two committed tables, through the reversal $V_b^\ast(X)=X^{N-1}V_b(1/X)$.
Because a smooth domain is closed under inversion, the verifier reads $V_b^\ast$ from the commitment to $b$ as a virtual word, and the Kronecker-FRI machinery (opening polynomial, virtual word, one folding test) proves $\sum_w a(w)b(w)=S$ with no sumcheck, with soundness error $3M/|\F|+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{fold}}+(1-\delta)^\kappa$.
With geometric weights $\gamma^w$ and DEEP quotients as further virtual words, the same method gives a sumcheck-free Hadamard check $a\circ b=c$ for three committed tables, with soundness error $2(N-1)/|\F|+8M/|\F|+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{fold}}+(1-\delta)^\kappa$. Finally, any list of evaluations, inner products and Hadamard checks on the same commitments is proved by one folding test, with soundness error $2(N-1)/|\F|+tM/|\F|+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{fold}}+(1-\delta)^\kappa$ for $t+1$ batched words; the term $2(N-1)/|\F|$ is paid once, whatever the number of Hadamard checks. The results of Sections~1--4 are machine-checked in Lean~4. Section~5 reduces sparse matrix--vector products, and hence R1CS, to the same statements with lookups by logarithmic derivatives, so an R1CS argument needs no sumcheck at all; its soundness and completeness are machine-checked in Lean~4 end to end. Measured on the same Kronecker-FRI commitment and proof engine, a sumcheck-free inner product costs the same as a sumcheck followed by a batched opening, a Hadamard check $1.7$ times more than a zerocheck, and the R1CS argument $19$ times more than the core of Spartan, which proves $2^{15}$ constraints on Kronecker-FRI in $0.29$\,s with $251$\,KiB proofs; the sumcheck-free arguments have a number of rounds independent of $n$ and a verifier that stays succinct.
\end{abstract}

\paragraph{Setting.}
Notation and parameters are those of Kronecker-FRI (KF)~\cite{KF}: $n\ge1$, $N=2^n$, a smooth domain $L\le\F^\times$ of order $M=2^{n+R}$ with $R\ge2$, codes $\Cc_j$, the folding test $\Pi_{\mathrm{FT}}[\ell,\kappa]$ with error $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{fold}}$ (KF Thm~5.24), fibre distance $\Delta^{\mathrm{fib}}_0$, and $0<\delta\le\delta^\ast=(1-\rho)/2$.
A bit vector $w\in\{0,1\}^n$ is identified with the integer $\sum_k w_k2^{k-1}\in\iv{0}{N-1}$.

\section{Table encoding and two extraction identities}

\begin{definition}[Table encoding, reversal]
For a table $f:\{0,1\}^n\to\F$, $V_f(X)=\sum_{w}f(w)X^{w}\in\F[X]_{<N}$ and $\mathrm{Enc}^{\mathrm T}(f)=\ev_L(V_f)$.
For $P\in\F[X]_{<N}$, its reversal is $P^\ast(X)=X^{N-1}P(1/X)$, so $\coef{j}{P^\ast}=\coef{N-1-j}{P}$ and $(P^\ast)^\ast=P$.
\end{definition}

$f\mapsto V_f$ is a linear bijection onto $\F[X]_{<N}$, so $\mathrm{Enc}^{\mathrm T}$ is a linear bijection onto $\Cc_0$ (as KF Lemma~6.3(1)); it is the Kronecker encoding of the multilinear polynomial whose coefficients are the values of $f$, and needs no M\"obius transform.
In particular $\mathrm{Enc}^{\mathrm T}(f)=\mathrm{Enc}(f)$ as maps on vectors of length $N$: a table-form commitment is an ordinary Kronecker-FRI commitment to the vector of values, with the same Merkle tree, and all of KF's results on commitments (unique decoding, extraction, batching) apply to it unchanged.

\begin{lemma}[Evaluation]\label{lem:eval}
For $z\in\F^n$ let $E^\ast_z(X)=\prod_{k=1}^n\bigl(z_k+(1-z_k)X^{2^{k-1}}\bigr)$. Then $\tilde f(z)=\coef{N-1}{V_f\,E^\ast_z}$, where $\tilde f$ is the multilinear extension of $f$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Expanding the product, $E_z(X):=\sum_w \mathrm{eq}(w,z)X^w=\prod_k\bigl((1-z_k)+z_kX^{2^{k-1}}\bigr)$, and $X^{N-1}E_z(1/X)=\prod_kX^{2^{k-1}}\bigl((1-z_k)+z_kX^{-2^{k-1}}\bigr)=E^\ast_z(X)$ since $N-1=\sum_k2^{k-1}$.
Hence $\coef{N-1-w}{E^\ast_z}=\coef{w}{E_z}=\mathrm{eq}(w,z)$ and $\coef{N-1}{V_fE^\ast_z}=\sum_wf(w)\,\mathrm{eq}(w,z)=\tilde f(z)$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Inner product]\label{lem:ip}
For tables $a,b$: $\sum_{w}a(w)b(w)=\coef{N-1}{V_a\,V_b^\ast}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$\coef{N-1}{V_aV_b^\ast}=\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\coef{i}{V_a}\coef{N-1-i}{V_b^\ast}=\sum_i\coef{i}{V_a}\coef{i}{V_b}=\sum_wa(w)b(w)$.
\end{proof}

Both are instances of one fact: for $U\in\F[X]_{<N}$ and a public $K\in\F[X]_{<N}$, $\coef{N-1}{UK}=\sum_i\coef{i}{U}\coef{N-1-i}{K}$.
KF's split lemma (Lemma~4.10) and identity lemma (Lemma~4.12) use only $\deg U,\deg K\le N-1$, so they hold verbatim for $UK$ replaced by $V_aE^\ast_z$ or $V_aV_b^\ast$:

\begin{lemma}[Split and identity, general form]\label{lem:gen-identity}
Let $U,K,A,H\in\F[X]_{<N}$ and $v\in\F$.
(1) There are $A,H\in\F[X]_{<N}$ with $XUK=A+vX^N+X^{N+1}H$ iff $v=\coef{N-1}{UK}$.
(2) $\Phi=XUK-A-vX^N-X^{N+1}H$ has degree at most $2N$, and $\Phi=0$ implies $v=\coef{N-1}{UK}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
As in KF Lemmas~4.10 and~4.12: $\deg(UK)\le2N-2$, and the three parts of the right-hand side occupy the disjoint degree ranges $\iv{0}{N-1}$, $\{N\}$, $\iv{N+1}{2N}$; compare the coefficients of $X^N$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Reversed words]\label{lem:rev}
For $w\in\F^L$ put $w^\ast(\xi)=\xi^{N-1}w(\xi^{-1})$. Then:
(1) $w=\ev_L(P)$ with $P\in\F[X]_{<N}$ implies $w^\ast=\ev_L(P^\ast)$; in particular $w\mapsto w^\ast$ maps $\Cc_0$ onto $\Cc_0$;
(2) $w\mapsto w^\ast$ is a linear bijection of $\F^L$, and $w^\ast=c^\ast$ at $\xi$ iff $w=c$ at $\xi^{-1}$;
(3) a set $T\subseteq L$ is fibre-closed iff $T^{-1}=\{\xi^{-1}:\xi\in T\}$ is, and $|T^{-1}|=|T|$;
(4) $h(\xi)=w^\ast(\xi)$ is computed from the value of $w$ at $\xi^{-1}\in L$, and a fold check at $\pm\xi$ reads $w$ on the fibre $\{\pm\xi^{-1}\}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$L$ is a group, so $\xi\mapsto\xi^{-1}$ is a bijection of $L$ mapping $-\xi$ to $-\xi^{-1}$ (as $-1\in L$); and $\xi^{N-1}\ne0$.
(1) $\xi^{N-1}P(\xi^{-1})=P^\ast(\xi)$, and $P\mapsto P^\ast$ is a bijection of $\F[X]_{<N}$. (2)--(4) follow from the first sentence.
\end{proof}

\section{The inner-product protocol}

\begin{definition}[$\Pi_{\mathrm{IP}}$]
Instance: oracles $w_a,w_b\in\F^L$ and $S\in\F$; honestly $w_a=\mathrm{Enc}^{\mathrm T}(a)$, $w_b=\mathrm{Enc}^{\mathrm T}(b)$ and $S=\sum_wa(w)b(w)$.
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item The prover sends $w_A=\ev_L(A)$, where $XV_aV_b^\ast=A+SX^N+X^{N+1}H$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:gen-identity}); the verifier sends $\beta\in\F$.
\item Put the virtual words $w_b^\ast$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:rev}) and
$h(\xi)=\bigl(\xi\,w_a(\xi)\,w_b^\ast(\xi)-w_A(\xi)-S\xi^N\bigr)/\xi^{N+1}$, and
\[w_0=w_a+\beta\,w_b^\ast+\beta^2\,w_A+\beta^3\,h.\]
\item Run $\Pi_{\mathrm{FT}}[\ell,\kappa]$ (or $\Pi^C_{\mathrm{FT}}$, KF Prop.~5.28) on $w_0$.
\end{enumerate}
Per query point the verifier reads $w_a(\pm\xi)$, $w_b(\pm\xi^{-1})$ and $w_A(\pm\xi)$, and does $O(1)$ extra field operations beyond KF (no kernel to evaluate).
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Completeness]
If $w_a=\ev_L(V_a)$, $w_b=\ev_L(V_b)$ and $S=\sum_wa(w)b(w)$, the honest prover is accepted with probability $1$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:ip} and Lemma~\ref{lem:gen-identity}(1), $A,H\in\F[X]_{<N}$ exist; by Lemma~\ref{lem:rev}(1) $w_b^\ast=\ev_L(V_b^\ast)$; evaluating the identity at $\xi$ gives $h=\ev_L(H)$. So $w_0=\ev_L(V_a+\beta V_b^\ast+\beta^2A+\beta^3H)$ with a polynomial of degree $<N$, and KF Lemma~5.9(1) applies.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Relation]
$\mathcal R^\delta_{\mathrm{IP}}$: $((w_a,w_b;S),(a,b))$ with $\Delta^{\mathrm{fib}}_0(w_a,\mathrm{Enc}^{\mathrm T}(a))\le\delta$, $\Delta^{\mathrm{fib}}_0(w_b,\mathrm{Enc}^{\mathrm T}(b))\le\delta$ and $\sum_wa(w)b(w)=S$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Batching, degree $3$]\label{lem:batch3}
Let $\mathcal G$ be the set of $\beta$ for which $w_0$ agrees with some $c\in\Cc_0$ on a fibre-closed $T\subseteq L$ with $|T|\ge(1-\delta)M$.
If $|\mathcal G|>3M$, then $(w_a,w_b;S)$ has a witness in $\mathcal R^\delta_{\mathrm{IP}}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The words $w_0$ form the curve of degree $3$ generated by $(w_a,w_b^\ast,w_A,h)$, and every $\beta\in\mathcal G$ has $\Delta(w_0,\Cc_0)\le\delta$.
By correlated agreement for curves (KF Thm~2.16, from~\cite{BCIKS23}, with $t=3$) there are $P_0,P_1,P_2,P_3\in\F[X]_{<N}$ and a set $\mathrm{Agr}$, $|\mathrm{Agr}|\ge(1-\delta)M$, on which $w_a=\ev(P_0)$, $w_b^\ast=\ev(P_1)$, $w_A=\ev(P_2)$, $h=\ev(P_3)$ (take $\mathrm{Agr}$ maximal).

\emph{Value.} Let $\Phi=XP_0P_1-P_2-SX^N-X^{N+1}P_3$. On $\mathrm{Agr}$, $\Phi(\xi)=\xi w_a(\xi)w_b^\ast(\xi)-w_A(\xi)-S\xi^N-\xi^{N+1}h(\xi)=0$ by the definition of $h$. Since $\deg\Phi\le2N<(1-\delta)M\le|\mathrm{Agr}|$ (KF Lemma~7.2, which uses $R\ge2$), $\Phi=0$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:gen-identity}(2) gives $S=\coef{N-1}{P_0P_1}$.
Let $a,b$ be the tables with $V_a=P_0$ and $V_b=P_1^\ast$; then $V_b^\ast=P_1$ and $S=\sum_wa(w)b(w)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:ip}.

\emph{Bad challenges.} For $\xi\notin\mathrm{Agr}$, $d_\xi(Z)=\sum_{i=0}^3Z^i(u_i-\ev(P_i))(\xi)$, with $(u_0,\dots,u_3)=(w_a,w_b^\ast,w_A,h)$, is a nonzero polynomial of degree $\le3$; its roots over all such $\xi$ form a set $\mathrm{Bad}$ with $|\mathrm{Bad}|\le3\delta M<3M<|\mathcal G|$.
Take $\beta\in\mathcal G\setminus\mathrm{Bad}$ with its $c$ and $T$, and $\hat c=\sum_i\beta^i\ev(P_i)\in\Cc_0$.
Then $c$ and $\hat c$ agree with $w_0$ on $T\cap\mathrm{Agr}$, of size $\ge(1-2\delta)M\ge N$, so $c=\hat c$; and for $\xi\in T$, $d_\xi(\beta)=w_0(\xi)-\hat c(\xi)=0$ forces $\xi\in\mathrm{Agr}$. So $T\subseteq\mathrm{Agr}$.

\emph{Distances.} $w_a=\ev(V_a)$ on the fibre-closed $T$, so $\Delta^{\mathrm{fib}}_0(w_a,\mathrm{Enc}^{\mathrm T}(a))\le\delta$ (KF Lemma~5.11(2)).
$w_b^\ast=\ev(V_b^\ast)=\ev(V_b)^\ast$ on $T$, so by Lemma~\ref{lem:rev}(2) $w_b=\ev(V_b)$ on $T^{-1}$, which is fibre-closed of the same size (Lemma~\ref{lem:rev}(3)); so $\Delta^{\mathrm{fib}}_0(w_b,\mathrm{Enc}^{\mathrm T}(b))\le\delta$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Soundness]
If $(w_a,w_b;S)$ has no witness in $\mathcal R^\delta_{\mathrm{IP}}$, every prover is accepted with probability at most
\[\frac{3M}{|\F|}+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{fold}}+(1-\delta)^\kappa\;<\;\frac{4M}{|\F|}+(1-\delta)^\kappa.\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Identical to KF Thm~7.8, with Lemma~\ref{lem:batch3} in place of KF Lemma~7.4: for $\beta\notin\mathcal G$ (probability $\ge1-3M/|\F|$) and good $r$ (probability $\ge1-\varepsilon_{\mathrm{fold}}$, KF Thm~5.24(1)), the codeword chain (KF Prop.~5.23) shows that $\mathrm{dens}_\ell(w_\ell)\ge1-\delta$ would put $\beta$ in $\mathcal G$, the witness set $\mathcal W_\ell(w_\ell)$ being fibre-closed (KF Lemma~7.7); so the query term is at most $(1-\delta)^\kappa$ (KF Lemma~3.3).
\end{proof}

Round-by-round knowledge soundness, the extractor (decode $w_a$ and $w_b$ at radius $\delta^\ast$) and binding follow as in KF Thm~7.11 and Cor.~7.12, with round-$1$ error $3M/|\F|$.

\begin{remark}[One commitment, three kinds of openings]
The same table-form commitment $\mathrm{Enc}^{\mathrm T}(f)$ supports: evaluation openings at any $z$ (KF's protocol with the kernel $E^\ast_z$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:eval}, evaluated by the verifier with $O(n)$ operations); hypercube sums ($z=(\tfrac12,\dots,\tfrac12)$ up to $2^n$, or the kernel $\prod_k(1+X^{2^{k-1}})$ directly); and inner products with any other committed table ($\Pi_{\mathrm{IP}}$).
Inner products with a \emph{public} table $p$ are the special case $K=V_p^\ast$, useful when $V_p^\ast(\xi)$ is cheap to evaluate (product form).
All of these batch with KF's batched openings (KF Thm~11.2).
\end{remark}

\section{A sumcheck-free Hadamard check}

SNARKs of Spartan/HyperPlonk type need, beyond linear functionals and inner products, the statement $a(w)b(w)=c(w)$ for all $w$ (a \emph{Hadamard check}), for three committed tables.

\paragraph{Geometric weights.} For $\gamma\in\F$,
\[
\sum_w\gamma^wa(w)b(w)=\coef{N-1}{V_a(\gamma X)\,V_b^\ast(X)},\qquad
\sum_w\gamma^wc(w)=V_c(\gamma),
\]
the first by Lemma~\ref{lem:ip} applied to the tables $w\mapsto\gamma^wa(w)$ and $b$.
If $d=a\circ b-c\ne0$, then $D_d(X)=\sum_wd(w)X^w$ is a nonzero polynomial of degree $\le N-1$, and the two sides differ unless $D_d(\gamma)=0$.
The values $V_a(\gamma\xi)$ and $V_c(\gamma)$ lie outside $L$; the prover commits to $Q(X)=V_a(\gamma X)$, and both are tied to the commitments by DEEP quotients, which are virtual words like $h$.

\begin{definition}[Quotient words]
For $w\in\F^L$ and $y,x\in\F$, put $q[w,y,x](\xi)=\bigl(w(\xi)-y\bigr)/(\xi-x)$, with the value $0$ at $\xi=x$ if $x\in L$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[DEEP step on an agreement set]\label{lem:deep}
Let $x,y\in\F$, $P,R\in\F[X]_{<N}$ and $T\subseteq L$ with $|T|>N+1$.
If $w=\ev(P)$ and $q[w,y,x]=\ev(R)$ on $T$, then $P(x)=y$ and $R=(P-y)/(X-x)$.
Conversely, if $x\notin L$, $w=\ev_L(P)$ and $P(x)=y$, then $q[w,y,x]=\ev_L\bigl((P-y)/(X-x)\bigr)$, a polynomial of degree $\le N-2$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
$(X-x)R-P+y$ has degree $\le N$ and vanishes on $T\setminus\{x\}$, which has more than $N$ points, so it is $0$; evaluate at $x$.
The converse is the factor theorem.
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[$\Pi_{\mathrm{Had}}$]\label{def:had}
Instance: oracles $w_a,w_b,w_c\in\F^L$; honestly $w_f=\mathrm{Enc}^{\mathrm T}(f)$ for $f\in\{a,b,c\}$ and $a\circ b=c$.
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item The verifier sends $\gamma$, uniform in $\F$. The prover sends $w_Q=\ev_L(Q)$, where $Q(X)=V_a(\gamma X)$.
\item The verifier sends $\theta$, uniform in $\F$. The prover sends $y_1=V_a(\gamma\theta)$, $y_3=V_c(\gamma)$ and $w_A=\ev_L(A)$, where $XQV_b^\ast=A+y_3X^N+X^{N+1}H$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:gen-identity}).
\item The verifier sends $\beta$, uniform in $\F$. With $h(\xi)=\bigl(\xi\,w_Q(\xi)\,w_b^\ast(\xi)-w_A(\xi)-y_3\xi^N\bigr)/\xi^{N+1}$ and
\[(u_0,\dots,u_8)=\bigl(w_a,\;w_c,\;w_Q,\;w_b^\ast,\;w_A,\;h,\;q[w_a,y_1,\gamma\theta],\;q[w_Q,y_1,\theta],\;q[w_c,y_3,\gamma]\bigr),\]
put $w_0=\sum_{i=0}^8\beta^iu_i$.
\item Run $\Pi_{\mathrm{FT}}[\ell,\kappa]$ (or $\Pi^C_{\mathrm{FT}}$) on $w_0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}
The challenges are unrestricted: soundness holds for all values, and a pole $\gamma\theta,\theta,\gamma$ in $L$ affects only completeness.
Using $y_1$ for both $V_a(\gamma\theta)$ and $Q(\theta)$ is the consistency check $V_a(\gamma\theta)=Q(\theta)$.
Per query point the verifier reads $w_a,w_c,w_Q,w_A$ on the coset of $\xi$ and $w_b$ on the coset of $\xi^{-1}$, and does $O(1)$ field operations and three inversions per point (one batched inversion per query) beyond the folding test.

\begin{theorem}[Completeness]
If $w_f=\mathrm{Enc}^{\mathrm T}(f)$ for $f\in\{a,b,c\}$ and $a\circ b=c$, the honest prover is accepted whenever $\gamma\theta,\theta,\gamma\notin L$, hence with probability at least $1-3M/|\F|$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
$Q\in\F[X]_{<N}$; $y_1=V_a(\gamma\theta)=Q(\theta)$; and $y_3=V_c(\gamma)=\sum_w\gamma^wc(w)=\sum_w\gamma^wa(w)b(w)=\coef{N-1}{QV_b^\ast}$, so $A,H$ exist and $h=\ev_L(H)$ as in $\Pi_{\mathrm{IP}}$.
By Lemma~\ref{lem:deep} the three quotient words are codewords when the poles lie outside $L$, and then $w_0$ is the codeword of a polynomial of degree $<N$.
Each of the events $\gamma\in L$, $\theta\in L$, $\gamma\theta\in L$ has probability at most $M/|\F|$ (for $\gamma\ne0$, at most $M$ values of $\theta$ put $\gamma\theta$ in $L$; for $\gamma=0$, none).
\end{proof}

\begin{definition}[Relation]
$\mathcal R^\delta_{\mathrm{Had}}$: $((w_a,w_b,w_c),(a,b,c))$ with $\Delta^{\mathrm{fib}}_0(w_f,\mathrm{Enc}^{\mathrm T}(f))\le\delta$ for $f\in\{a,b,c\}$ and $a\circ b=c$.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Batching, degree $8$]\label{lem:batch8}
Fix $\gamma,\theta\in\F$ and the prover's $w_Q,y_1,y_3,w_A$.
Let $\mathcal G$ be the set of $\beta$ for which $w_0$ agrees with some $c'\in\Cc_0$ on a fibre-closed $T\subseteq L$ with $|T|\ge(1-\delta)M$.
If $|\mathcal G|>8M$, there are tables $a,b,c$ and $\hat Q\in\F[X]_{<N}$ with
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item $\Delta^{\mathrm{fib}}_0(w_f,\mathrm{Enc}^{\mathrm T}(f))\le\delta$ for $f\in\{a,b,c\}$, and $\Delta^{\mathrm{fib}}_0(w_Q,\ev_L(\hat Q))\le\delta$;
\item $V_a(\gamma\theta)=y_1=\hat Q(\theta)$;
\item $V_c(\gamma)=y_3=\coef{N-1}{\hat Q\,V_b^\ast}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Correlated agreement on one fibre-closed set (the step shared with Lemma~\ref{lem:batch3}; Lean: \texttt{curve\_agreement}, $t=8$) gives $P_0,\dots,P_8\in\F[X]_{<N}$ and a fibre-closed $T$, $|T|\ge(1-\delta)M>2N\ge N+2$ (KF Lemma~7.2), with $u_i=\ev(P_i)$ on $T$.
As in Lemma~\ref{lem:batch3}, $XP_2P_3-P_4-y_3X^N-X^{N+1}P_5$ vanishes on $T$ and has degree $\le2N$, so $y_3=\coef{N-1}{P_2P_3}$.
Lemma~\ref{lem:deep} with $(P_0,P_6,\gamma\theta)$, $(P_2,P_7,\theta)$ and $(P_1,P_8,\gamma)$ gives $P_0(\gamma\theta)=y_1$, $P_2(\theta)=y_1$ and $P_1(\gamma)=y_3$.
Let $V_a=P_0$, $V_c=P_1$, $V_b=P_3^\ast$ and $\hat Q=P_2$; then (2) and (3) hold, and (1) follows from agreement on $T$ (for $w_b$, on $T^{-1}$, Lemma~\ref{lem:rev}).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Soundness]\label{thm:had}
Let $R\ge2$ and $0<\delta\le\delta^\ast$. If $(w_a,w_b,w_c)$ has no witness in $\mathcal R^\delta_{\mathrm{Had}}$, every prover is accepted with probability at most
\[
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Had}}=\frac{2(N-1)}{|\F|}+\frac{8M}{|\F|}+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{fold}}+(1-\delta)^\kappa .
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
For $\delta\le\delta^\ast$ each word has at most one codeword within fibre distance $\delta$ (KF Lemma~6.3(2)), so the tables and $\hat Q$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:batch8} are the decodings of $w_a,w_b,w_c,w_Q$.
It suffices to consider deterministic provers (a randomised prover is an average of deterministic ones), so $w_Q$ is a function of $\gamma$ and the round-$2$ messages are functions of $(\gamma,\theta)$.
Call $(\gamma,\theta)$ \emph{good} if, for every choice of the prover's round-$2$ messages, $|\mathcal G|\le8M$. For good $(\gamma,\theta)$ the argument of KF Thm~7.8 applied to the rest of the protocol bounds the acceptance probability by $8M/|\F|+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{fold}}+(1-\delta)^\kappa$. It remains to bound the probability that $(\gamma,\theta)$ is not good.

\emph{Case 1: some $w_f$ has no table within fibre distance $\delta$.} Lemma~\ref{lem:batch8}(1) fails, so every $(\gamma,\theta)$ is good.

\emph{Case 2: $w_a,w_b,w_c$ decode to $\hat a,\hat b,\hat c$ with $\hat a\circ\hat b\ne\hat c$.}
Let $D=D_{\hat a\circ\hat b-\hat c}\ne0$. The event $D(\gamma)=0$ has probability at most $(N-1)/|\F|$. Fix $\gamma$ with $D(\gamma)\ne0$, and the prover's $w_Q$ (a function of $\gamma$).
If $w_Q$ has no codeword within fibre distance $\delta$, $(\gamma,\theta)$ is good for every $\theta$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:batch8}(1).
If it decodes to $\hat Q=V_{\hat a}(\gamma X)$, then Lemma~\ref{lem:batch8}(3) would give $\sum_w\gamma^w\hat c(w)=V_{\hat c}(\gamma)=\coef{N-1}{V_{\hat a}(\gamma X)V_{\hat b}^\ast}=\sum_w\gamma^w\hat a(w)\hat b(w)$, i.e.\ $D(\gamma)=0$; so every $\theta$ is good.
Otherwise $\hat Q-V_{\hat a}(\gamma X)$ is a nonzero polynomial of degree $\le N-1$, fixed before $\theta$ is drawn; the event $\hat Q(\theta)=V_{\hat a}(\gamma\theta)$ has probability at most $(N-1)/|\F|$, and outside it Lemma~\ref{lem:batch8}(2) fails, so $\theta$ is good.
\end{proof}

So $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Had}}<(8M+2N)/|\F|+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{fold}}+(1-\delta)^\kappa$, against $3M/|\F|$ for $\Pi_{\mathrm{IP}}$ and $2M/|\F|$ for an evaluation.
The curve has degree $8$ because $w_a$ and $w_c$ must be in the batch themselves: a quotient word alone determines $w_a$ on $T$ only as a polynomial of degree $\le N$, which does not identify the decoding.

Knowledge follows: if the acceptance probability exceeds $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Had}}$, the instance has a witness, and the extractor decodes $w_a,w_b,w_c$ at radius $\delta$ (KF Cor.~7.12).

\begin{remark}[Consequence]
Linear functionals (evaluations, with the kernel $E^\ast_z$), inner products ($\Pi_{\mathrm{IP}}$) and Hadamard checks ($\Pi_{\mathrm{Had}}$) are all proved on table-form Kronecker-FRI commitments by one folding test each, with no sumcheck and a verifier that is an FRI verifier plus $O(1)$ operations per query point (plus $O(n)$ for $E^\ast_z$).
For R1CS-type SNARKs what remains is the sparse matrix--vector products $Az,Bz,Cz$, which Spartan handles with its own sparse-polynomial commitment; whether those admit a coefficient-extraction form is the next question.
\end{remark}

\section{One folding test for a batch of statements}\label{sec:batch}

A SNARK needs many statements at once on the same commitments: Hadamard checks, inner products and evaluations.
Since every statement of Sections~1--3 is checked by words in one curve, all of them can share one curve and one folding test.

\begin{definition}[Statements]
Let $w_1,\dots,w_r\in\F^L$ be commitments. A \emph{statement} is one of the following, with indices in $\{1,\dots,r\}$:
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item $\mathsf{Eval}(i,z,v)$ with $z\in\F^n$, $v\in\F$: the table $f_i$ of $w_i$ has $\tilde f_i(z)=v$;
\item $\mathsf{IP}(i,i',S)$ with $S\in\F$: $\sum_wf_i(w)f_{i'}(w)=S$;
\item $\mathsf{Had}(i,i',k)$: $f_i\circ f_{i'}=f_k$.
\end{itemize}
For a list $\Sigma=(\sigma_1,\dots,\sigma_J)$ of $J\ge1$ statements, let $D$ be the set of commitment indices read directly ($i$ in $\mathsf{Eval}$ and $\mathsf{IP}$; $i,k$ in $\mathsf{Had}$), $B$ the set of indices read reversed ($i'$ in $\mathsf{IP}$ and $\mathsf{Had}$), and $\mathcal H\subseteq\{1,\dots,J\}$ the positions of the Hadamard checks. A table may play several roles.
The relation $\mathcal R^\delta_{\mathrm{Batch}}$ holds for $((w_i)_i,\Sigma)$ and tables $(f_i)_{i\in D\cup B}$ if $\Delta^{\mathrm{fib}}_0(w_i,\mathrm{Enc}^{\mathrm T}(f_i))\le\delta$ for all $i\in D\cup B$ and every statement of $\Sigma$ holds for these tables.
\end{definition}

For each statement $\sigma_j$ the protocol uses a triple $(U_j,K_j,v_j)$, with $U_j,K_j\in\F[X]_{<N}$, and the identity $XU_jK_j=A_j+v_jX^N+X^{N+1}H_j$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:gen-identity}:
$(V_{f_i},E^\ast_z,v)$ for $\mathsf{Eval}(i,z,v)$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:eval});
$(V_{f_i},V_{f_{i'}}^\ast,S)$ for $\mathsf{IP}(i,i',S)$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:ip});
$(Q_j,V_{f_{i'}}^\ast,y_3^{(j)})$ with $Q_j(X)=V_{f_i}(\gamma X)$ for $\sigma_j=\mathsf{Had}(i,i',k)$ (Section~3).
The verifier reads $U_j(\xi)$ and $K_j(\xi)$ as $w_i(\xi)$ and $E^\ast_z(\xi)$, as $w_i(\xi)$ and $w_{i'}^\ast(\xi)$, or as $w_Q^{(j)}(\xi)$ and $w_{i'}^\ast(\xi)$.

\begin{definition}[$\Pi_{\mathrm{Batch}}$]
Instance: $(w_i)_i$ and $\Sigma$.
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item The verifier sends $\gamma$, uniform in $\F$. For each $j\in\mathcal H$ the prover sends $w_Q^{(j)}=\ev_L(Q_j)$.
\item The verifier sends $\theta$, uniform in $\F$. For each $j\in\mathcal H$, with $\sigma_j=\mathsf{Had}(i,i',k)$, the prover sends $y_1^{(j)}=V_{f_i}(\gamma\theta)$ and $y_3^{(j)}=V_{f_k}(\gamma)$; for each $j\le J$ it sends $w_A^{(j)}=\ev_L(A_j)$.
\item The verifier sends $\beta$, uniform in $\F$. The words are, in a fixed order: $w_i$ for $i\in D$; $w_i^\ast$ for $i\in B$; $w_Q^{(j)}$ for $j\in\mathcal H$; $w_A^{(j)}$ and $h_j(\xi)=\bigl(\xi\,U_j(\xi)K_j(\xi)-w_A^{(j)}(\xi)-v_j\xi^N\bigr)/\xi^{N+1}$ for $j\le J$; and $q[w_i,y_1^{(j)},\gamma\theta]$, $q[w_Q^{(j)},y_1^{(j)},\theta]$, $q[w_k,y_3^{(j)},\gamma]$ for $j\in\mathcal H$, $\sigma_j=\mathsf{Had}(i,i',k)$.
They are $t+1=|D|+|B|+2J+4|\mathcal H|$ words $u_0,\dots,u_t$; put $w_0=\sum_{s=0}^t\beta^su_s$.
\item Run $\Pi_{\mathrm{FT}}[\ell,\kappa]$ (or $\Pi^C_{\mathrm{FT}}$) on $w_0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}
$\gamma$ and $\theta$ are shared by all Hadamard checks. All $w_Q^{(j)}$ can be committed in one Merkle tree whose leaves hold one value of each, and likewise all $w_A^{(j)}$; a query then opens, beyond the folding test, one path per commitment in $D$, one per commitment in $B$ (on the inverse coset), and at most two paths for the prover's oracles (one if $\mathcal H=\emptyset$), whatever $J$.

\begin{theorem}[Completeness]
If $w_i=\mathrm{Enc}^{\mathrm T}(f_i)$ and every statement holds, the honest prover is accepted whenever $\mathcal H=\emptyset$ or $\gamma\theta,\theta,\gamma\notin L$; so with probability at least $1-3M/|\F|$.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Each $w_A^{(j)}$, $h_j$ and quotient word is a codeword, by the completeness proofs of Sections~2 and~3 and Lemma~\ref{lem:eval} for $\mathsf{Eval}$ (where $E^\ast_z$ is a public polynomial of degree $<N$).
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Batching, degree $t$]\label{lem:batchT}
Fix $\gamma,\theta$ and the prover's messages, and let $\mathcal G$ be the set of $\beta$ for which $w_0$ agrees with some $c'\in\Cc_0$ on a fibre-closed $T\subseteq L$ with $|T|\ge(1-\delta)M$.
Let $R\ge2$ and $0<\delta\le\delta^\ast$. If $|\mathcal G|>tM$, there are tables $(f_i)_{i\in D\cup B}$ and polynomials $\hat Q_j\in\F[X]_{<N}$ ($j\in\mathcal H$) such that $\Delta^{\mathrm{fib}}_0(w_i,\mathrm{Enc}^{\mathrm T}(f_i))\le\delta$, $\Delta^{\mathrm{fib}}_0(w^{(j)}_Q,\ev_L(\hat Q_j))\le\delta$, and
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item every $\mathsf{Eval}$ and $\mathsf{IP}$ statement of $\Sigma$ holds for the $f_i$;
\item for every $j\in\mathcal H$ with $\sigma_j=\mathsf{Had}(i,i',k)$: $V_{f_i}(\gamma\theta)=y_1^{(j)}=\hat Q_j(\theta)$ and $V_{f_k}(\gamma)=y_3^{(j)}=\coef{N-1}{\hat Q_jV_{f_{i'}}^\ast}$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Correlated agreement on one fibre-closed set (\texttt{curve\_agreement} with this $t$) gives polynomials of degree $<N$ for all the words and a fibre-closed $T$ with $|T|\ge(1-\delta)M>2N$ on which all words agree with them.
Tables: $V_{f_i}$ is the polynomial of $w_i$ for $i\in D$; for $i\in B$, $V_{f_i}$ is the reversal of the polynomial of $w_i^\ast$, and $w_i$ agrees with $\mathrm{Enc}^{\mathrm T}(f_i)$ on $T^{-1}$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:rev}). If $i\in D\cap B$, both give tables within fibre distance $\delta$ of $w_i$, which coincide by unique decoding.
For each $j$, the identity of $h_j$ on $T$ and Lemma~\ref{lem:gen-identity}(2) give $v_j=\coef{N-1}{U_jK_j}$ with the polynomials of the words; for $\mathsf{Eval}$ this is $v=\tilde f_i(z)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:eval}, for $\mathsf{IP}$ it is $S=\sum_wf_i(w)f_{i'}(w)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:ip}, and for $\mathsf{Had}$ it is the second equality of (2) with $\hat Q_j$ the polynomial of $w_Q^{(j)}$.
The three quotient words of each $j\in\mathcal H$ give the other equalities of (2) by Lemma~\ref{lem:deep}, as in Lemma~\ref{lem:batch8}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Soundness]\label{thm:batch}
Let $R\ge2$ and $0<\delta\le\delta^\ast$. If $((w_i)_i,\Sigma)$ has no witness in $\mathcal R^\delta_{\mathrm{Batch}}$, every prover is accepted with probability at most
\[
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Batch}}=\frac{2(N-1)}{|\F|}+\frac{tM}{|\F|}+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{fold}}+(1-\delta)^\kappa.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
As for Theorem~\ref{thm:had}: call $(\gamma,\theta)$ good if $|\mathcal G|\le tM$ for all round-$2$ messages; for good $(\gamma,\theta)$ the acceptance probability is at most $tM/|\F|+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{fold}}+(1-\delta)^\kappa$.
If some $w_i$, $i\in D\cup B$, has no table within fibre distance $\delta$, every pair is good by Lemma~\ref{lem:batchT}.
Otherwise let $\hat f_i$ be the decodings; some statement $\sigma_{j^\ast}$ is false for them, and $j^\ast$ depends only on the instance.
If $\sigma_{j^\ast}$ is an $\mathsf{Eval}$ or $\mathsf{IP}$ statement, Lemma~\ref{lem:batchT}(1) fails, so every pair is good.
If $\sigma_{j^\ast}=\mathsf{Had}(i,i',k)$, the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:had} applies verbatim to this one check, with Lemma~\ref{lem:batchT}(2) for $j^\ast$ in place of Lemma~\ref{lem:batch8}: the pair is good unless $D_{\hat f_i\circ\hat f_{i'}-\hat f_k}(\gamma)=0$, or $\hat Q_{j^\ast}\ne V_{\hat f_i}(\gamma X)$ and $\hat Q_{j^\ast}(\theta)=V_{\hat f_i}(\gamma\theta)$, events of probability at most $(N-1)/|\F|$ each.
\end{proof}

When $\mathcal H=\emptyset$ the same proof shows that every pair is good, so the first term can be omitted (this refinement is not in the Lean formalisation).
Only one false statement is needed in the proof, so the term $2(N-1)/|\F|$ from $\gamma,\theta$ is paid once, not once per Hadamard check; the term $tM/|\F|$ grows linearly in the number of words, by $4M/|\F|$ per Hadamard check and $2M/|\F|$ per other statement. For $|\F|=p^2\approx2^{128}$, $M=2^{22}$ and $t=100$, this term is about $2^{-99}$.
Knowledge follows as before: if the acceptance probability exceeds $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Batch}}$, the instance has a witness, obtained by decoding each $w_i$.

\section{Sparse matrix--vector products without sumcheck}\label{sec:lin}

An R1CS instance asks for $z$ with $(Az)\circ(Bz)=Cz$ for public sparse matrices $A,B,C$.
Committing to $z$ and to $a=Az$, $b=Bz$, $c=Cz$, the statement $a\circ b=c$ is a Hadamard check.
This section proves $a=\mathbf Mz$ for a public sparse matrix $\mathbf M$ (a \emph{lincheck}) with the statements of Section~\ref{sec:batch} on affine forms of committed and public tables; lookups are handled with logarithmic derivatives (LogUp, Hab\"ock~\cite{Hab22}).
Throughout, $N$ is at least the length of $z$ and the number of nonzero entries of each matrix, and all tables are padded to length $N=2^n$; $R\ge2$ and $0<\delta\le\delta^\ast$. Several matrices $\mathbf M_1,\dots,\mathbf M_J$ are handled together (for R1CS, $J=3$), and $\operatorname{char}\F>JN$.

\subsection{Affine forms and public tables}

A \emph{public table} is a table $P$ whose polynomial $V_P$ (and hence $V_P^\ast$) the verifier evaluates at any point with few operations. We use
\[
\mathbf 1(w)=1,\qquad \mathbf 0(w)=0,\qquad \mathrm{id}(w)=w,\qquad G_\eta(w)=\eta^{w}\ (0^0=1),
\]
with $w$ read as an integer in $\iv{0}{N-1}$ and then as an element of $\F$, for which $V_{\mathbf 1}(X)=\prod_{k<n}(1+X^{2^k})=V_{\mathbf 1}^\ast(X)$, $V_{G_\eta}(X)=V_{\mathbf 1}(\eta X)$, $V_{G_\eta}^\ast(X)=\prod_{k<n}(X^{2^k}+\eta^{2^k})$, and $V_{\mathrm{id}}=T_n$ with $S_0=1$, $T_0=0$, $S_{k+1}=(1+X^{2^k})S_k$, $T_{k+1}=(1+X^{2^k})T_k+2^kX^{2^k}S_k$; each costs $O(n)$ operations.
An \emph{affine form} is $\lambda=\sum_{i\in S}c_iw_i+\sum_jd_jP_j$ with a finite set $S$ of commitments (its \emph{support}, listed in the instance; a coefficient $c_i$ may vanish), public tables $P_j$ and coefficients $c_i,d_j\in\F$. The form is \emph{public} if $S=\emptyset$. Its word at $\xi$ is $\sum_ic_iw_i(\xi)+\sum_jd_jV_{P_j}(\xi)$, and for tables $f_i$ of the $w_i$ its table is $\sum_ic_if_i+\sum_jd_jP_j$.

\begin{proposition}[$\Pi_{\mathrm{Batch}}$ on affine forms]\label{prop:affine}
Let the arguments of the statements of Section~\ref{sec:batch} be affine forms, the coefficients and the parameters of the public tables being part of the instance.
\begin{itemize}[nosep]
\item \emph{Relation:} tables are required for the committed words in the support of some argument, within fibre distance $\delta$; every statement holds for the tables of its affine forms. Public tables carry no condition.
\item \emph{Curve:} the committed words occurring directly, the reversals of those occurring reversed, every $w_Q^{(j)}=\ev_L(V_{\lambda}(\gamma X))$ (public part included), every $w_A^{(j)}$ and $h_j$, and the quotient words $q[\lambda,y,x]$ of the forms $\lambda$ that are not public. When the form of a quotient word is public, the verifier checks $y=V_\lambda(x)$ directly instead.
\end{itemize}
Then completeness, Lemma~\ref{lem:batchT} and Theorem~\ref{thm:batch} hold with $t+1$ the number of words in the curve. The verifier's work per query point grows by $O(n)$ per public table.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
On the agreement set $T$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:batchT}, the word of an affine form agrees with the polynomial $\sum_ic_iP_{w_i}+\sum_jd_jV_{P_j}$ of degree $<N$, since public tables agree with their polynomials everywhere. The proofs of Lemma~\ref{lem:batchT} and Theorem~\ref{thm:batch} use only such agreements, Lemmas~\ref{lem:gen-identity} and~\ref{lem:deep}, and the decodings of the committed words, which determine the tables of the affine forms; the case analysis of Theorem~\ref{thm:had} uses only the polynomial $V_{\hat\lambda}$ of the decoded form. A direct check $y=V_\lambda(x)$ gives the conclusion of Lemma~\ref{lem:deep} for a public form.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The lincheck}

\begin{definition}[Index, relation]
A sparse $\mathbf M_j\in\F^{N\times N}$ ($j<J$) with at most $N$ nonzero entries is listed as $(r_{j,k},c_{j,k},v_{j,k})_{k<N}$, padded with $v_{j,k}=0$, $r_{j,k}=c_{j,k}=0$. The \emph{index} of $(\mathbf M_j)_{j<J}$ consists of the commitments $\mathrm{Enc}^{\mathrm T}$ of the tables $\mathrm{row}_j(k)=r_{j,k}$, $\mathrm{col}_j(k)=c_{j,k}$, $\mathrm{val}_j(k)=v_{j,k}$ and of the \emph{combined} multiplicities $m_R(w)=\#\{(j,k):r_{j,k}=w\}$, $m_C(w)=\#\{(j,k):c_{j,k}=w\}$, computed once by an honest indexer (soundness uses only that the $\mathrm{row}_j,\mathrm{col}_j,\mathrm{val}_j$ and $m_R,m_C$ are honest), all in one Merkle tree.
The lincheck relation holds for commitments $(w_z,(w_{a_j})_j)$ and tables $(z,(a_j)_j)$ if all are within fibre distance $\delta$ of $\mathrm{Enc}^{\mathrm T}$ of the tables and $a_j=\mathbf M_jz$ for every $j$.
\end{definition}

The reduction rests on $\sum_w\eta^w(\mathbf M_jz)(w)=\sum_kv_{j,k}\,\eta^{r_{j,k}}z(c_{j,k})$: the prover commits to $e_{j,k}=\eta^{r_{j,k}}$, $\zeta_{j,k}=z(c_{j,k})$ and $p_{j,k}=e_{j,k}\zeta_{j,k}$, and proves the $JN$ lookups $(r_{j,k},e_{j,k})\in\{(w,\eta^w)\}$ with \emph{one} LogUp, and the $JN$ lookups $(c_{j,k},\zeta_{j,k})\in\{(w,z(w))\}$ with another.

\begin{definition}[$\Pi_{\mathrm{Lin}}$]\label{def:lin}
Instance: the index of $(\mathbf M_j)_{j<J}$ and commitments $w_z,(w_{a_j})_j$.
\begin{enumerate}[nosep]
\item The verifier sends $\eta$, uniform in $\F$. The prover commits to tables $e_j,\zeta_j,p_j$ (honestly $e_{j,k}=\eta^{r_{j,k}}$, $\zeta_{j,k}=z(c_{j,k})$, $p_{j,k}=e_{j,k}\zeta_{j,k}$).
\item The verifier sends $x_R,y_R,x_C,y_C$, uniform in $\F$. The prover commits to tables $\phi^R_j,\psi^R,\phi^C_j,\psi^C$ and sends $s_j\in\F$; honestly
\begin{align*}
&\phi^R_{j,k}=\frac1{x_R-r_{j,k}-y_Re_{j,k}},&& \psi^R_w=\frac{m_R(w)}{x_R-w-y_R\eta^w},\\
&\phi^C_{j,k}=\frac1{x_C-c_{j,k}-y_C\zeta_{j,k}},&& \psi^C_w=\frac{m_C(w)}{x_C-w-y_Cz(w)},
\end{align*}
with $\psi_w=0$ when $m(w)=0$; the honest prover aborts if a denominator with $m(w)\ne0$ vanishes.
\item The statements, proved by one $\Pi_{\mathrm{Batch}}$ on affine forms (Proposition~\ref{prop:affine}) whose own challenges $\gamma,\theta,\beta$ are drawn after step~2: for every $j$,
\begin{align*}
&\mathsf{IP}(a_j,G_\eta,s_j),\quad \mathsf{IP}(\mathrm{val}_j,p_j,s_j),\quad \mathsf{Had}(e_j,\zeta_j,p_j),\\
&\mathsf{Had}(\phi^R_j,\,x_R\mathbf 1-\mathrm{row}_j-y_Re_j,\,\mathbf 1),\quad \mathsf{Had}(\phi^C_j,\,x_C\mathbf 1-\mathrm{col}_j-y_C\zeta_j,\,\mathbf 1),
\end{align*}
and once
\begin{align*}
&\mathsf{Had}(\psi^R,\,x_R\mathbf 1-\mathrm{id}-y_RG_\eta,\,m_R),\quad \textstyle\mathsf{IP}(\sum_j\phi^R_j-\psi^R,\mathbf 1,0),\\
&\mathsf{Had}(\psi^C,\,x_C\mathbf 1-\mathrm{id}-y_Cz,\,m_C),\quad \textstyle\mathsf{IP}(\sum_j\phi^C_j-\psi^C,\mathbf 1,0),
\end{align*}
that is $5J+4$ statements.
\end{enumerate}
\end{definition}

The honest prover aborts with probability at most $2N/|\F|$ (a denominator $x-w-y\,\cdot$ with $m(w)\ne0$ vanishes; the denominators of the $\phi$ are among them), and is otherwise accepted with the probability of Proposition~\ref{prop:affine}, at least $1-3M/|\F|$.

\begin{lemma}[LogUp with pairs]\label{lem:logup}
Let pairs $(\alpha_k,\alpha'_k)_{k<K}$ and $(\omega_w,\omega'_w)_{w<N_t}$ in $\F^2$, $m_w\in\F$, and $\operatorname{char}\F>K$. If some $(\alpha_{k_0},\alpha'_{k_0})$ is not among the $(\omega_w,\omega'_w)$, then for $x,y$ uniform in $\F$,
\[
\Pr\Bigl[\textstyle\sum_k\frac1{x-\alpha_k-y\alpha'_k}=\sum_w\frac{m_w}{x-\omega_w-y\omega'_w}\text{ with all denominators nonzero}\Bigr]\le\frac{K+2N_t-1}{|\F|}.
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix $y$ and write $c_k=\alpha_k+y\alpha'_k$, $t_w=\omega_w+y\omega'_w$. The equation $c_{k_0}=t_w$ is $\alpha_{k_0}-\omega_w=y(\omega'_w-\alpha'_{k_0})$; it has no solution $y$ when $\alpha'_{k_0}=\omega'_w$ (then $\alpha_{k_0}\ne\omega_w$) and one otherwise, so $c_{k_0}$ is a table value $t_w$ for at most $N_t$ values of $y$.
For the other $y$, let $S=\{c_k\}$, $n_c=\#\{k:c_k=c\}$ and
\[
\mathrm{Num}(X)=\sum_{c\in S}n_c\!\!\prod_{c'\in S\setminus\{c\}}\!\!(X-c')\prod_w(X-t_w)-\sum_wm_w\prod_{c\in S}(X-c)\prod_{v\ne w}(X-t_v),
\]
of degree $\le K+N_t-1$. At $X=c_{k_0}$ only the term of $c=c_{k_0}$ survives, and it is $n_{c_{k_0}}\in\iv1K$ (nonzero in $\F$) times nonzero products, so $\mathrm{Num}\ne0$. Where all denominators are nonzero, the equation multiplied by $\prod_{c\in S}(x-c)\prod_w(x-t_w)$ is $\mathrm{Num}(x)=0$, which holds for at most $K+N_t-1$ values of $x$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Lincheck reduction]\label{thm:lin}
Suppose the index is honest, the decodings $z,a_j$ of $w_z,w_{a_j}$ exist and $a_{j_0}\ne\mathbf M_{j_0}z$ for some $j_0$, and fix a deterministic prover. Let $E$ be the event, over $\eta,x_R,y_R,x_C,y_C$, that every word committed by the prover in steps~1--2 has a decoding within fibre distance $\delta$ and that these decodings, with $z,a_j$ and the index, satisfy all the statements of step~3. Then
\[
\Pr[E]\le\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Lin}}=\frac{N-1}{|\F|}+2\,\frac{N+(JN+2N-1)}{|\F|}=\frac{(2J+7)N-3}{|\F|}.
\]
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
On $E$: from $\mathsf{Had}(\phi^R_j,x_R\mathbf 1-\mathrm{row}_j-y_Re_j,\mathbf 1)$, every $x_R-r_{j,k}-y_Re_{j,k}$ is nonzero and $\phi^R_{j,k}$ is its inverse. From $\mathsf{Had}(\psi^R,\dots,m_R)$, $\psi^R_w(x_R-w-y_R\eta^w)=m_R(w)$; unless $x_R=w+y_R\eta^w$ for some $w$ (probability $\le N/|\F|$ over $x_R$, for every $y_R$), $\psi^R_w=m_R(w)/(x_R-w-y_R\eta^w)$. The last row statement gives $\sum_{j,k}\phi^R_{j,k}=\sum_w\psi^R_w$.
The decodings $e_j$ are fixed before $x_R,y_R$, and $w\mapsto w\in\F$ is injective on $\iv0{N-1}$ since $\operatorname{char}\F>N$; so by Lemma~\ref{lem:logup} with $K=JN$ and $N_t=N$, except with probability $(JN+2N-1)/|\F|$, every $(r_{j,k},e_{j,k})$ is a pair $(w,\eta^w)$, that is $e_{j,k}=\eta^{r_{j,k}}$.
In the same way, except with probability $(N+JN+2N-1)/|\F|$, $\zeta_{j,k}=z(c_{j,k})$ for all $j,k$ (the decodings $\zeta_j$ and $z$ are fixed before $x_C,y_C$).
Then $\mathsf{Had}(e_{j_0},\zeta_{j_0},p_{j_0})$ gives $p_{j_0,k}=\eta^{r_{j_0,k}}z(c_{j_0,k})$, and the first two statements give
$\sum_w\eta^wa_{j_0}(w)=s_{j_0}=\sum_kv_{j_0,k}\eta^{r_{j_0,k}}z(c_{j_0,k})=\sum_w\eta^w(\mathbf M_{j_0}z)(w)$.
So $\eta$ is a root of the nonzero polynomial $\sum_w(a_{j_0}-\mathbf M_{j_0}z)(w)X^w$ of degree $\le N-1$, fixed before $\eta$: probability $\le(N-1)/|\F|$.
\end{proof}

For $J=1$, $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Lin}}=(9N-3)/|\F|$. Separate lookups for each matrix would cost $J(4N-1)/|\F|$ per kind of lookup instead of $((J+3)N-1)/|\F|$, and $J-1$ more words $\psi$ and more statements per kind.

\begin{theorem}[Soundness of the lincheck]\label{thm:linsound}
If the index is honest and $(w_z,(w_{a_j})_j)$ has no witness for the lincheck relation, every prover of $\Pi_{\mathrm{Lin}}$ followed by $\Pi_{\mathrm{Batch}}$ is accepted with probability at most $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Lin}}+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Batch}}$, with $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Batch}}$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:batch} for the $t+1$ words of step~3.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
It suffices to consider deterministic provers. Let $\tau$ be the transcript of steps~1--2; the batch instance $I(\tau)$ and the event $E$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:lin} are functions of $\tau$, and given $\tau$ the rest of the prover is a prover of $\Pi_{\mathrm{Batch}}$ facing fresh challenges.
If $w_z$ or some $w_{a_j}$ has no decoding, $I(\tau)$ has no witness for every $\tau$. Otherwise $a_{j_0}\ne\mathbf M_{j_0}z$ for some $j_0$, and for $\tau\notin E$, $I(\tau)$ has no witness: by unique decoding a witness would consist of the decodings, which either do not exist or violate a statement.
In both cases Theorem~\ref{thm:batch} gives $\Pr[\text{accept}\mid\tau]\le\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Batch}}$ for $\tau\notin E$, so the acceptance probability is at most $\Pr[E]+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Batch}}$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{R1CS}

\begin{definition}[R1CS relation]
Public: sparse $A,B,C\in\F^{N\times N}$ (with an honest index), input positions $I\subseteq\iv0{N-1}$ and an input $x\in\F^I$. Let $\hat x$ be the table equal to $x$ on $I$ and $0$ elsewhere, and $\chi$ the $0/1$ table of $I$ (both public tables; for $I=\iv0{|I|-1}$, $V_\chi(X)=(X^{|I|}-1)/(X-1)$, and in general $V_{\hat x},V_\chi$ cost $O(|I|)$).
The relation holds for commitments $(w_w,w_a,w_b,w_c)$ and tables $(w,a,b,c)$ if all four are within fibre distance $\delta$, $w$ vanishes on $I$, and with $z=\hat x+w$: $a=Az$, $b=Bz$, $c=Cz$ and $a\circ b=c$.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[R1CS without sumcheck]\label{thm:r1cs}
Run $\Pi_{\mathrm{Lin}}$ for $(\mathbf M_0,\mathbf M_1,\mathbf M_2)=(A,B,C)$ and $(a_0,a_1,a_2)=(a,b,c)$ (so $J=3$ and $\operatorname{char}\F>3N$) on the affine form $z=\hat x+w_w$, and prove its $19$ statements, $\mathsf{Had}(a,b,c)$ and $\mathsf{Had}(w,\chi,\mathbf 0)$ in one $\Pi_{\mathrm{Batch}}$ on affine forms. If the instance has no witness, every prover is accepted with probability at most
\[
\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Lin}}+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Batch}}\le\frac{15N-5+tM}{|\F|}+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{fold}}+(1-\delta)^\kappa ,
\]
with $t+1=127$ words: $23$ read directly, $17$ reversed, $13$ words $w_Q$, $21$ words $w_A$, $21$ words $h$ and $32$ quotient words, for every instance and every value of the challenges.
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
As in Theorem~\ref{thm:linsound}, with one false statement: if some decoding is missing, the batch instance never has a witness; if $a\ne Az$, $b\ne Bz$ or $c\ne Cz$ for the decodings, only the event $E$ of the lincheck matters, of probability $\le\varepsilon_{\mathrm{Lin}}=(13N-3)/|\F|$; otherwise $a\circ b\ne c$ or $w$ does not vanish on $I$, and $\mathsf{Had}(a,b,c)$ or $\mathsf{Had}(w,\chi,\mathbf 0)$ is false for every value of the challenges.
\end{proof}

\emph{Completeness.} If $w_w=\mathrm{Enc}^{\mathrm T}(w)$ and $w_{a_j}=\mathrm{Enc}^{\mathrm T}(a_j)$ for a witness, the honest prover is accepted with probability at least $1-2N/|\F|-3M/|\F|$: outside the challenges at which some $x_R-w-y_R\eta^w$ or $x_C-w-y_Cz(w)$ vanishes (probability $\le2N/|\F|$), the honest tables satisfy the $21$ statements (the two sum statements by $\sum_{j,k}f(r_{j,k})=\sum_wm_R(w)f(w)$ and its column analogue), and Proposition~\ref{prop:affine} applies.

Every step is a coefficient extraction, a lookup by logarithmic derivatives, or a Hadamard check: an R1CS argument on Kronecker-FRI commitments needs no sumcheck.

\paragraph{Status.} The algebra of $\Pi_{\mathrm{Lin}}$ is checked in the clear by the Rust module \texttt{lincheck} (closed forms of $V_{\mathbf 1}$, $V_{\mathrm{id}}$, $V_{G_\eta}$; honest runs with padded matrices; a wrong $a$ caught by the weighted sums; a forged $\zeta_k$ or $e_k$ that repairs the weighted sums caught by the column or row lookup).
The compiled protocol is implemented: the module \texttt{affine} runs $\Pi_{\mathrm{Batch}}$ on affine forms (Proposition~\ref{prop:affine}) with committed words grouped in Merkle trees whose leaves hold one value of each word of a group, and the module \texttt{r1cs} implements the indexer, prover and verifier of Theorem~\ref{thm:r1cs}: one index tree of $11$ words ($\mathrm{row}_j,\mathrm{col}_j,\mathrm{val}_j$, $m_R$, $m_C$), combined row and column lookups, $21$ statements and $t+1=127$ words (the words of a form are its declared support, so the count does not depend on the challenges; test \texttt{word\_count\_127}, the count of the Lean theorem \texttt{tA\_r1stmts}); the proof opens six trees per query (witness, index, the two lincheck groups, the two groups of $\Pi_{\mathrm{Batch}}$). Its tests accept honest proofs and reject a wrong public input, an unsatisfied constraint, a witness that is nonzero on an input position, a false lincheck alone, and a false lincheck repaired by a forged $\zeta_k$ or $e_k$; removing the sum statement of the column (row) lookup makes the forged $\zeta_k$ ($e_k$) accepted, so each lookup is exercised.
The Lean formalisation of this section is described in Section~\ref{sec:status}.

\section{Kronecker-FRI in sumcheck-based arguments: the two routes measured}\label{sec:routes}

The protocols of Sections~2--5 are one of two ways to use Kronecker-FRI for statements on committed tables; the other is a sumcheck, which reduces the statement to the values of the tables at one random point, followed by an opening. This section measures both on the same commitment.

\paragraph{Post-quantum setting.} Kronecker-FRI is hash-based: its security rests on the hash function and on the proximity gaps of Reed--Solomon codes, with no discrete-logarithm or pairing assumption. Every argument built on it is post-quantum in this sense, the sumcheck route included, since a sumcheck is an information-theoretic reduction. The sumcheck-free protocols are therefore an alternative to the sumcheck with the same security basis; they change the cost, the number of rounds and the verifier, not the security assumptions.

\paragraph{One statement.} The Rust module \texttt{sumcheck} proves one inner product and one Hadamard check by both routes with the same commitments (one tree per table) and the same proof engine (\texttt{affine}): the \emph{sumcheck-free} route proves $\mathsf{IP}(a,b,S)$ or $\mathsf{Had}(a,b,c)$; the \emph{sumcheck} route runs a sumcheck of degree~$2$ for the inner product, or the zerocheck $\sum_x\mathrm{eq}(\tau,x)(a(x)b(x)-c(x))=0$ of degree~$3$ for the Hadamard check, and then proves \emph{one} evaluation statement on the affine form $\sum_i\rho^iw_i$ at the sumcheck point, a batched opening. Only the reduction differs. Tests accept honest proofs of both routes and reject a wrong sum, a false Hadamard instance and wrong values at the sumcheck point.

\section{Relation to known techniques and status}\label{sec:status}

Writing an inner product as the middle coefficient of a product with a reversed polynomial is classical, and appears in pairing-based multilinear commitments (Mercury~\cite{EG25} uses the table form with KZG).
DEEP quotients for out-of-domain values are standard in FRI-based STARKs.
The new points here are: (1) the reversed virtual word $w_b^\ast$, which reads the second vector from the \emph{same} Reed--Solomon commitment thanks to $L^{-1}=L$; (2) the reuse of Kronecker-FRI's opening polynomial, virtual word and single folding test, with a fully proved soundness bound (Lemma~\ref{lem:batch3}); (3) the table-form evaluation kernel $E^\ast_z$, which gives evaluation openings on the same commitments; (4) a Hadamard check in which the out-of-domain values are DEEP quotient words inside the same batch as the opening polynomial, so that one degree-$8$ curve and one folding test carry the whole argument (Lemma~\ref{lem:batch8}).

\paragraph{Prover cost and the sumcheck baseline.}
The prover of $\Pi_{\mathrm{IP}}$ computes one product $V_aV_b^\ast$ of two polynomials of degree $<N$ over the base field (three NTTs of size $2N$), one NTT of size $M$ and one Merkle tree for $w_A$, and then runs the folding test; every round-$1$ object lies in the base field, since $V_a$, $V_b$ do.
The natural baseline is the sumcheck route: $n$ rounds of a degree-$2$ sumcheck on $\sum_wa(w)b(w)$, followed by openings of $a$ and $b$ at the random point.
For that baseline the fastest prover we know of in our own work is the tree circuit of~\cite{MI26}, which produces the evaluations, hypercube sums and sumcheck round polynomials of a multilinear polynomial from one binary-tree recursion; Section~\ref{sec:routes} compares the two routes on the same commitment and engine.

An inner product of two committed tables costs less prover time than one evaluation opening (the round-$1$ work is in the base field), about the same verifier time, and about $20\%$ more proof, the extra openings of $w_b$ on the inverse cosets.
A Hadamard check costs about $2.2$ evaluation openings of prover time (its polynomials $Q$, $A$, $H$ lie in the extension field) and $1.7$ times the proof, since five oracles are opened per query instead of two; when $a$, $b$, $c$ are committed together, one tree with three values per leaf removes two of the five authentication paths per query.
